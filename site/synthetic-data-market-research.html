What synthetic data means in market research

Synthetic data is any research result that was produced by a system rather than collected from a live respondent. A synthetic respondent answers a survey. A synthetic audience shows who a plan would reach. A digital twin does both, and can also react to a video.

The word covers very different things. Some synthetic data is a statistical copy of an old survey, made so the raw file can be shared without exposing anyone. Some is a chatbot told to answer as "a 34-year-old mother in Ohio." Some is built from the record of what real people did. These three are not equally useful, and a buyer should always ask which one is in front of them.

The three ways synthetic data gets made

The input decides the output. A twin built from surveys can tell you what people say. A twin built from behavior can tell you what people do.

Why synthetic data built on behavior is better

Synthetic data can only be as good as what goes into it. Surveys and chatbots put in words. Crosswalk puts in five years of what 10 million real people did.

  • Behavior is what you pay to predict. Budgets depend on signups, views, cancels and sales. A behavior-built twin learns from exactly those.
  • People say one thing and do another. Someone who says price does not matter may still cancel the week it goes up. A survey records the words; the panel records the cancel.
  • Real people set the shape. Every share traces back to opted-in US people, not to a chatbot's idea of a type.
  • Yesterday is included. The panel refreshes every morning, so a twin knows last week's premiere and yesterday's price change.
  • Each forecast can be checked. A forecast built on behavior can be scored against behavior. That is why every Crosswalk call is published before the result.

Why buyers are using synthetic data now

Speed and cost. A survey takes weeks and a focus group takes a room, a recruiter and a moderator. A synthetic read takes minutes, and you can ask the next question right away.

In a May 2026 Qualtrics survey, 41% of market researchers said they already use synthetic data to add to or replace respondents. In the same survey, 62% said they plan to. The buyer's question has moved from "should we" to "which kind, and how do we check it."

Six checks before you trust a synthetic number

Ask these six questions of any vendor, including Crosswalk. A vendor that cannot answer them is selling an opinion.

  1. What is it built from? Surveys, a model, or observed behavior. Ask to see the source and how many people are behind it.
  2. Was the answer checked against real outcomes, or against another survey? Matching a survey proves the twin can repeat what people say. Matching a real result (viewers, signups, votes, sales) proves it can predict what people do.
  3. Were the calls dated before the result? A forecast written after the outcome is not a forecast. Look for a public record with dates and the misses kept on it.
  4. Does every answer show a range and say how firm it is? A bare percentage hides how sure the system is. A good vendor tells you when a read is firm and when it is early.
  5. Can it be scored going forward? Ask for a call on something that has not happened yet, and check it when it lands.
  6. Is it disclosed? The ICC/ESOMAR International Code (2025 edition) requires researchers to tell clients when synthetic data or synthetic personas are used. It also asks how much human oversight there was. Your own report should say what rests on real people and what rests on twins.

FAQ Questions people ask

Plain answers on synthetic data.

What is synthetic data in market research?

Research results produced by a system rather than collected from a live respondent. The source can be past surveys, a chatbot acting out a type of person, or the recorded actions of real people. The input decides what the output can tell you.

Why is Crosswalk's synthetic data more reliable?

Because what goes in is real behavior, not answers or guesses. The twins draw on 5+ years of behavior from 10 million opted-in US people, refreshed every morning. Every call is written down before the result and scored when it lands.

Is synthetic data based on real data?

It depends on the vendor. Crosswalk's is. Its twins are built from years of observed behavior on 10 million opted-in US people. The inputs are real; the output is what those people would do with something they have not seen yet.

How do you validate synthetic data?

Date the call before the result. Score it against a real outcome, not another survey. Keep the misses. Show a range and how firm each count is. Crosswalk does all four on a public track record.

Are synthetic respondents accurate?

Survey-built ones can repeat what people say fairly well. Model-built ones tend to answer too consistently and miss the extremes. Behavior-built twins learn from what people really did, so they can be scored on the same thing.

Do I have to disclose synthetic data to my stakeholders?

Yes. The ICC/ESOMAR International Code (2025 edition) requires researchers to tell clients when synthetic data or synthetic personas are used. It also asks how much human oversight there was. Your report should say which findings rest on real people and which rest on twins.

Can synthetic data replace a survey?

For questions about what people will do, a behavior-built twin is often a better instrument than a survey, because it is scored against outcomes. For how people describe their own feelings, a survey still has a place.

What is a synthetic audience?

The twins a plan would reach, sized, described and projected to the US population. At Crosswalk a synthetic audience is made of 12 to 16 archetypes that add up to the whole audience.
