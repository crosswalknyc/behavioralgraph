Methodology Digital Twins IQ

How Crosswalk's digital twins work.

A digital twin is a copy of an audience that you can ask questions. Crosswalk builds each twin from 5+ years of what 10 million real people did online. Inside every twin are at least 12 archetypes: groups of people who act alike. In a focus group, one person speaks for each group. You ask a question and see how many people would pick each answer, and how sure we are.

How it works in four steps

You pick an audience, we build its twin, you ask it something, and you get the answer with the people behind it.

  1. Pick the audience. The people who watch a show, use a service, follow a star or buy a brand. Or describe a group yourself, such as women 25 to 44 in Dallas.
  2. We build the twin. We look at what those people did online over the past 5+ years, then find the archetypes inside the audience.
  3. Ask it something. Ask questions in a focus group, bring one business question, or show it a video.
  4. Get the answer. You see what share of the audience picked each answer, how many people that is, why they picked it, and how firm the answer is.

Why this method is stronger

Most twins are built from what people say in a survey or an interview, or from a chatbot playing a type of person. Ours are built from what people did.

  • Real people. 10 million US people stand behind the twins, about 1 in 33 Americans. A typical survey hears from a few thousand.
  • Years of history. We have more than 2,000 days of what the same people did. A survey or an interview captures one moment.
  • Every screen. Phone, laptop and TV count as one person, so we see the search, the show and the purchase that came after.
  • Up to date. New behavior arrives every morning.
  • Groups found in the data. The audience decides its own archetypes. Nobody hand-picks personas.
  • Answers in real people. Every share comes with the number of US people behind it.
  • A public record. We write down each prediction before the result and keep every miss on a public page.

Where a twin comes from

Every twin starts with real behavior. Crosswalk's panel is 10 million US people who chose to be counted, and we see their whole digital life.

That means what they watch and stream, what they search for, what they buy, and what they sign up for or cancel. We see it on every screen, day by day, since January 2021. A twin is built from that history.

Three ways to build one

  • An audience we already know. The viewers of a show, the fans of a star, the users of a service or the buyers of a brand. If we do not have it yet, we build it first, usually in under an hour.
  • A group you describe. Pick gender, age, income, ethnicity and where people live. You can set the shares yourself.
  • A list of links. Paste links to a show page, a channel, a feed or an article. The twin is the people who went there.

Finding the archetypes

We sort the audience into groups of people who act alike. Every twin gets at least 12 groups, and it can have more.

First we sort people by where they are with the subject. There are five stages: just found it, trying it, in the habit, drifting away, and back after a break. Then we sort by age. Very small groups join the group closest to them. If fewer than 12 groups are left, we split the biggest ones, for example by gender or age.

Each archetype gets a plain name and one representative to speak for it. Here are four of the 16 archetypes in the Amazon Prime Video twin, which stands for 128,116,302 US subscribers.

What happens in the focus group

The focus group has one seat for each archetype. Every representative answers in their own words, and each answer counts for the people behind it.

  • At least 12 seats. There is one seat per archetype, so the audience sets the number. You never pick it.
  • It looks like the audience. If a twin is 72% women, about 9 of 12 seats go to women.
  • Bigger groups count more. Devin speaks for 16.6 million people and Leah for 3.6 million. His answer counts about 4.6 times as much as hers.
  • Representatives remember. They recall what they said earlier, in this group and in past groups on the same twin, as a real person would.

When you ask a question

  1. The focus group first learns the facts about your subject: what it is, when it came out and where it plays.
  2. Each representative answers in two or three sentences, based on what their group did before. They also say how sure they are, from 1 to 5.
  3. We add up the people behind each answer.
  4. You get a short summary, the quotes that explain it, and two questions worth asking next.

Example: one question on the Prime Video twin, November 18, 2026

"Which of these would get you most excited if it finally became a show?"

The answer: 63.1% of subscribers, about 80.8 million people, picked "a game I play." Nine of the 16 seats chose it. Those seats speak for bigger groups, so the share of people is higher than the share of seats.

Kenji, 33, chose the game. "A game I play, no contest, because Fallout did exactly that for me."

Devin, 65, chose a book series. "A game means nothing to me at my age."

Three words that tell you how sure we are

Every answer comes with a range and one of three words. The word tells you how close the real number should be to ours.

WordHow closeWhat it means
FirmWithin about 5 pointsWe are sure of this one.
LeaningWithin about 12 pointsA lean, not a lock.
EarlyUp to about 20 pointsThe range is wide, but you still get an answer.

In the example above, the 63.1% was leaning. The real share should fall between 51.2% and 75.0%.

One business question, answered by Predictive Analytics

Bring one business question. Prometheus, our research assistant, writes 5 to 8 questions, asks them in a focus group, and gives you a verdict with a full report.

Your question can take almost any shape. It might be "Will this win?", "Should we do this?" or "What will people pay?" The plan starts wide, with where the audience stands today. Then it narrows to what pulls people in and what holds them back. Last comes your question itself. Planning is free, and you can edit or drop any question first.

Example: the Prime Video run, November 18, 2026

The question: which new show or sport, found on no other service, would get people to pay for Prime Video? The verdict: a new weeknight live sports package. 55.7% of subscribers, about 71.3 million people, picked it. That is about 4 times the 14.1% who said nothing would get them to pay.

Pilot Testing for shows and ads

Pilot Testing shows your video to the focus group. Each representative watches it, reacts, answers questions, and fills out a scorecard at the end.

  • Add your video. A pilot, an episode, an ad or a film, from 15 seconds to 4 hours. Upload it or paste a link.
  • We learn who is on screen. Faces are matched to your cast list. Only people on your list are ever named.
  • Each representative turns a dial. The dial runs from 0 to 100 and moves as they watch, with a reading every 2 to 4 seconds. A 43-minute pilot gets 706 readings per representative.
  • You can ask questions as it plays. Questions come at the turns of the story, and you can add your own at any moment.
  • A scorecard comes at the end. It asks what studios ask after a screening: the rating, and whether people would recommend it, finish it and come back for the next episode.
  • Then a forecast. How many people would watch in week one, how many would finish the season, and how many would sign up to watch it.

After the test you can play the dial back to any second and see what was on screen and what people said. A test screening needs a recruiter, a room and a week. Pilot Testing needs the file.

What you get

Every result comes back as files you can share: a CSV, a PDF and, for twins and Pilot Testing, a deck.

When you finish a focus group, Prometheus also emails you a PDF of the session with a link back to it.

FAQ Questions people ask about the method

The methodology, in plain words.

What is a Crosswalk digital twin built from?

From what real people did. Crosswalk sees the whole digital life of 10 million US people who chose to be counted, day by day since January 2021. No surveys, no interviews, and no chatbot guessing at a type of person.

How many archetypes does a twin have?

At least 12, and there can be more. The audience decides the number. If an audience has fewer than 12 clear groups, we split the biggest ones until there are 12.

Why does one answer count more than another?

Representatives speak for groups of different sizes. One may speak for 16.6 million people and another for 3.6 million. The bigger group's answer counts for more, so the result matches the real audience.

What do firm, leaning and early mean?

They tell you how close the real number should be. Firm means within about 5 points. Leaning means within about 12. Early means a wider range, up to about 20 points.

How accurate are Crosswalk's digital twins?

We publish every prediction before the result and score it after. On the first six, all three winners were called right, and our numbers were off by 3.2% on average.

Do the answers change over time?

They can, because people change. A twin keeps learning from what its people do, so a question asked next month may get a different answer. The answer follows the audience.

What can Pilot Testing watch?

A pilot, an episode, an ad, a film or a run of episodes, from 15 seconds to 4 hours. Upload an MP4, MOV, WebM or M4V file, or paste a link.

Is any person identified?

No. We only answer about groups, never about one person. Identity is kept apart from behavior, and clients see group results scaled up to the US population.
