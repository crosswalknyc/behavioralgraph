Comparison Four ways to build a twin

Synthetic respondents vs behavioral digital twins.

Four kinds of product now call themselves digital twins or synthetic respondents. They are built from different things, so they answer different questions. Interview twins and survey twins repeat what people say. Model-built personas repeat the average of the internet. Census-built populations match the country's shape but have never watched anything. Behavior-built twins, the kind Crosswalk makes, start from what real people did, so they can be scored against what people do next. That is the kind to use.

Why saying and doing are different data

People are honest and still wrong about themselves. They say they will subscribe for a show and then watch it on a friend's account. They say price does not matter and then cancel the week it goes up.

A twin built from surveys inherits every one of those gaps, because surveys are its only source. A twin built from a language model inherits the gaps in the text it was trained on. It also tends to answer too consistently and squeeze the extremes. A twin built from behavior starts with the record of what happened: the signup, the cancel, the second episode, the order placed. Those are the things a spend decision depends on.

This is also why the two kinds are checked differently. A survey twin is validated by matching a survey. A behavior twin is validated by matching an outcome. Only the second kind of check tells you whether the forecast would have saved you money.

Ten reasons Crosswalk's twins are stronger

Every Crosswalk twin is behavior-built. Here is what that gives you, in ten points.

  1. Real people stand behind every answer. The panel holds 10 million opted-in US people, about 1 in 33 Americans. A chatbot persona stands on no one.
  2. Five years of history. More than 2,000 days of what the same people did, in order, with timing. A survey or an interview captures one moment.
  3. The whole digital life. Search, streaming, social, shopping, apps and gaming on phone, laptop and TV, all under one ID.
  4. Fresh every morning. Yesterday's behavior is in today's answer. A chatbot knows the world as of its training.
  5. Answers counted in people. Each answer carries the real people behind its seat, so every share comes with a US head count.
  6. The audience builds the room. At least 12 archetypes come from the data, sized by their people. No one hand-picks personas.
  7. One business question, a full plan. Predictive Analytics writes 5 to 8 questions from wide to narrow, runs them, and returns a verdict with a report.
  8. Seats with a memory. Each archetype representative remembers what it said in your earlier rooms on that twin, as a real person would.
  9. It watches your cut. Pilot Testing reads every seat's dial every 2 to 4 seconds. That is 706 readings per seat on a 43-minute pilot, with faces matched to your cast list.
  10. Scored in public, against real results. Every call is dated before the outcome. On the first six, all three winners were called right and the counts landed within 3.2% on average.

Seven questions to ask any twin vendor

The answers sort the four kinds in a single call.

  1. What is the twin built from, and how many real people are behind it?
  2. Do you validate against surveys or against outcomes? Show me one of each.
  3. Are your calls dated before the result? Where can I read the misses?
  4. Does every answer carry a range, and say how firm it is?
  5. How fresh is the data behind an answer today?
  6. Can the twin watch my cut and tell me the second it lost the room?
  7. Will you put a call on something that has not happened yet, so I can check it myself?

FAQ Questions people ask

Synthetic respondents and digital twins, in plain words.

What is a synthetic respondent?

A program that answers a survey as if it were a person. Most are built from past surveys and interviews, or from a language model told to answer as a type of person. They report what people say.

What is a behavioral digital twin?

A copy of a real audience built from what its people did over time. Crosswalk's twins run on 5+ years of recorded behavior from 10 million opted-in US people and are scored against real outcomes.

Are Crosswalk's twins better than interview-built twins?

For predicting what people will do, yes. An interview-built twin knows one conversation with a person. A Crosswalk twin knows years of what millions of people did, and its calls are scored against real outcomes in public.

Which is more accurate, synthetic respondents or behavioral twins?

Behavioral twins. A synthetic respondent can only repeat what people say, and people often act differently. A behavior-built twin learns from what people did: what they watched, paid for, cancelled and came back to. Crosswalk publishes how its calls landed.

Why do behavior-built twins predict better?

Actions are a better guide to the next action than words are. A behavior-built twin learns from signups, cancels, second episodes and orders. Those are the same things a forecast is judged on.

Where do interview twins come from?

A twin is built from a long interview with one real person, so it can answer later surveys the way that person would. A well-known 2024 Stanford study reproduced people's survey answers at about 85% of their own two-week retest this way.

How does a census-built synthetic population work?

A set of made-up people with real census traits (zip code, age, income, job), sometimes fed current news, used to answer poll-shaped questions. It matches the country's shape but has never watched a show or paid for a service.

Can a language model be a digital twin?

Not a reliable one. A language model can play a type of person, but it has never met your audience. It tends to answer too consistently, and it cannot be scored against what your audience did.

How do I know if a vendor's twin is behavior-built?

Ask what it is built from and how it is validated. A behavior-built twin names a consented panel, validates against outcomes rather than surveys, dates its calls before the result, and shows you the misses.

Do I still need a survey?

No. A Crosswalk twin tells you what people will do, and its focus groups tell you why, in each representative's own words. Every answer rests on real behavior, not on what people remember or plan to do.
