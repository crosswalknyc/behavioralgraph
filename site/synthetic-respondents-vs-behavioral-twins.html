Comparison Four ways to build a twin

Synthetic respondents vs behavioral digital twins.

Four kinds of product now call themselves digital twins or synthetic respondents. They are built from different things, so they answer different questions. Interview twins and survey twins repeat what people say. Model-built personas repeat the average of the internet. Census-built populations match the country's shape but have never watched anything. Behavior-built twins, the kind Crosswalk makes, start from what real people did, so they can be scored against what people do next.

Why saying and doing are different data

People are honest and still wrong about themselves. They say they will subscribe for a show and then watch it on a friend's account. They say price does not matter and then cancel the week it goes up.

A twin built from surveys inherits every one of those gaps, because surveys are its only source. A twin built from a language model inherits the gaps in the text it was trained on. It also tends to answer too consistently and squeeze the extremes. A twin built from behavior starts with the record of what happened: the signup, the cancel, the second episode, the order placed. Those are the things a spend decision depends on.

This is also why the two kinds are checked differently. A survey twin is validated by matching a survey. A behavior twin is validated by matching an outcome. Only the second kind of check tells you whether the forecast would have saved you money.

When to use which

Use the instrument that matches the question. Most teams end up using more than one.

  • How people describe a brand, or which of two taglines sounds better. An interview twin or a survey twin is fine. The question is about words.
  • Twenty ideas need a first pass before anyone spends time on them. A model-built persona can rank them in an afternoon. Treat the output as a sketch, not a number.
  • A poll-shaped read on the whole country. A census-built population gives you the right shape of respondents, and can be checked against polls.
  • Money rides on how many people will watch, finish, pay, return or leave. Use a behavior-built twin, and ask to see its scored calls.

What makes a Crosswalk twin behavior-built

Every Crosswalk twin is built from the record of what its people did on an opted-in panel of 10 million US people. It is made of the archetypes inside the audience.

  • One ID per person across every screen. Phone, laptop and TV, in order, with the time of day, since January 2021.
  • 12 to 16 archetypes per twin. Each has a person who speaks for it, and each answer counts for the people behind it. Read what a digital twin audience is.
  • Twelve habits, observed. Reacts to price, cancels when the title ends, comes back for the next entry, buys the product, phone in hand while watching. Seven more, each a dial from 0 to 100.

Seven questions to ask any twin vendor

The answers sort the four kinds in a single call.

  1. What is the twin built from, and how many real people are behind it?
  2. Do you validate against surveys or against outcomes? Show me one of each.
  3. Are your calls dated before the result? Where can I read the misses?
  4. Does every answer carry a range and a confidence score?
  5. How fresh is the data behind an answer today?
  6. Can the twin watch my cut and tell me the second it lost the room?
  7. Will you put a call on something that has not happened yet, so I can check it myself?

FAQ Questions people ask

Synthetic respondents and digital twins, in plain words.

What is a synthetic respondent?

A program that answers a survey as if it were a person. Most are built from past surveys and interviews, or from a language model told to answer as a type of person. They report what people say.

What is a behavioral digital twin?

A copy of a real audience built from what its people actually did over time. Crosswalk's twins run on 5+ years of recorded behavior from 10 million opted-in US people and are scored against real outcomes.

Which is more accurate, synthetic respondents or behavioral twins?

It depends on the question. For what people will say, a survey-built twin can be close. For what people will do (watch, pay, cancel, return), only a behavior-built twin can be checked against the real result, and Crosswalk publishes those checks.

Where do interview twins come from?

A twin built from a long interview with one real person, so it can answer later surveys the way that person would. A well-known 2024 Stanford study reproduced people's survey answers at about 85% of their own two-week retest this way.

How does a census-built synthetic population work?

A set of made-up people with real census traits (zip code, age, income, job), sometimes fed current news, used to answer poll-shaped questions. It matches the country's shape but has never watched a show or paid for a service.

Can a language model be a digital twin?

It can play a type of person, which is useful for a quick sketch. It has never met your audience, it tends to answer too consistently, and it cannot be scored against what your audience did. Treat its output as a draft, not a count.

How do I know if a vendor's twin is behavior-built?

Ask what it is built from and how it is validated. A behavior-built twin names a consented panel, validates against outcomes rather than surveys, dates its calls before the result, and shows you the misses.

Can I use both kinds?

Yes, and many teams do. Use a survey-built twin for how people describe a brand, and a behavior-built twin for how many will watch, pay or leave.
