02 How a call is scored

Three steps, the same for every count.

1. The call is dated

When a room publishes a split or a pilot test publishes a week-one figure, the record keeps the question, the audience, the figure and its range, and the time, with a hash of all of it. The hash cannot be made after the fact.

2. The outcome lands

A certified result, a published figure, or our own count of what the audience did is written next to the call. A count made after the outcome was already known is marked and never scored.

3. The miss is kept

How far the figure was off, whether the outcome fell inside the range given, and whether the side we called was the side that won. The misses stay on the page. A scored miss is worth more than a soft number.

03 How firm a count is

Every count carries a range and one of three words.

The word comes from what the count rests on, how far the room agreed by the people its seats stand for, how steady the count's own range was, how calls like it have scored on this page, and how much was on file about the thing asked. The room always makes the call; the word says how firm it is.

Firm

The room is sure of this one. The count rests on something counted, or the record shows calls like it land close. The range is tight.

Leaning

A lean, not a lock. The count rests on what was found about the thing asked and on how the room answered; the range is wider.

Early

An early call. The room still names who leads and by how much; the range is wide, and it tightens as calls like it settle on this page.
