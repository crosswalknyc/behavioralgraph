Guide AI focus groups

How an AI focus group works.

An AI focus group is a room of digital twins that you can question the way you would question real people. In Crosswalk's Focus Group IQ, the room seats one person for each archetype in the audience, never fewer than 12. Every seat speaks in character from its people's real history, then the count adds up the real people behind every answer. You get a share, a people count, a range, a word for how firm it is, and the voices behind the result. A traditional group takes weeks to recruit and speaks only for the people in its chairs. This room runs in minutes and speaks for the whole audience.

What an AI focus group is

A traditional focus group puts 8 to 12 recruited people in a room with a moderator for two hours. An AI focus group puts the archetypes of an audience in a room, and each one has a seat that answers for it.

The point is the same: hear the audience talk, and count where it lands. The difference is who is in the room and what they answer from. In a Crosswalk room, the seats are archetype representatives built from 5+ years of what real people did. They do not have to be recruited, they are never tired, and every one of them stands for a known number of real people.

Who sits in the room

One seat per archetype. The audience decides how many that is.

A Crosswalk twin is made of at least 12 archetypes, the groups inside the audience. Each archetype has a person who speaks for it, with a name, an age, a city and a bio. The room seats all of them. When an audience has fewer than 12 clear groups, Crosswalk splits the largest ones further, by gender or age for example. The room always has at least 12 voices.

The room looks like the real audience. A twin that is 72% women seats about 9 women out of 12. An archetype that is gay men or trans women is seated as exactly that, because that is who the archetype is. Each seat remembers what it said earlier in the room, and in past rooms you have used it in, the way a real person would.

Example: the Amazon Prime Video room

16 seats standing for 128,116,302 US subscribers. Devin, 65, Tampa, stands for 16,572,733 older regulars on sport and crime series. Kenji, 33, New York, stands for 14,605,258 under-35 fans of The Boys and Fallout. Keisha, 35, Atlanta, stands for 7,046,397 parents who stopped after the season ended. How a question runs

Four steps, the same for every question.

  1. The room gets the facts. Before anyone answers, the room learns what you are asking about. It learns what it is, when it came out, where it plays, and anything else the seats would naturally know. It also learns how similar things did before with each archetype.
  2. Each seat answers in character. Two or three sentences, from who it is and its known history. Then it gives an answer and says how sure it is from 1 to 5, and why.
  3. The count adds up people. Each answer carries the people behind its seat. A seat that stands for 16.6 million people counts for more than one that stands for 3.6 million. Shares add to 100% and people add to the room.
  4. You get the read. A short paragraph in plain words, the voices that tell the story, and two suggested questions worth asking next.

One question, November 18, 2026

"Which of these would get you most excited if it finally became a show?" Lead answer: a game I play. 9 of 16 seats picked it, standing for 61.8% of the audience. The room's count: 63.1%, or 80,841,387 people, with a range of 51.2% to 75.0%. The word on the count was leaning. Kenji: "A game I play, no contest, because Fallout did exactly that for me." Devin: "A game means nothing to me at my age."

The count adds up people, not seats

Nine of sixteen seats is 56%. The room's count was 63.1%. The gap is the people.

In a traditional focus group, every chair counts the same, so a room of twelve can over-weight a small slice of the audience. In a Crosswalk room, each seat carries the real people behind its archetype. The seat tally always sits beside the count so you can see both. The count also takes in what is known about the question. That is why it can sit a few points away from the raw people-weighted tally.

The answers rest on what people did, not guesses. Ask again next month and the count can move, because the audience's behavior moved.

How firm each count is

Every count carries a range and one of three words. The room always makes the call; the word says how firm it is.

WordRange around the countWhat it means
FirmWithin about 5 pointsThe room is sure of this one. The count rests on something counted, or calls like it have landed close.
LeaningWithin about 12 pointsA lean, not a lock. The count rests on what was found about the subject and how the room answered.
EarlyUp to about 20 pointsAn early call. The room still names who leads and by how much, and the range tightens as calls like it settle.

When you have a business question, not a list of questions

Predictive Analytics writes the questions for you. Bring one business question. Prometheus plans 5 to 8 questions from wide to narrow, runs the room, and hands back a verdict with a report.

The plan runs in three steps. The big picture comes first: where the audience stands today. Then closer in: what pulls them and what holds them back. Last comes the question itself, put to every seat. Planning is free, and you can edit or drop any question before it runs. The report gives the verdict, the numbers behind it, why, who drove it, what would change it, and what to ask next. It comes as a page and a PDF.

What an AI focus group can and cannot tell you

It can tell you what an audience will most likely do and why, in minutes, with a stated range. It cannot tell you a private feeling that never showed up in behavior.

  • Good at: which option an audience picks, and who picks it. What pulls them and what holds them back. How a price or a change lands, and how firm the read is.
  • Also good at: watching a cut. Pilot Testing seats the same room in front of a video. A dial reads every 2 to 4 seconds, and a scorecard comes at the end.
  • Not for: questions about one named person, or feelings that leave no trace in what people watch, search, buy or cancel.

Every room comes back as a CSV and a PDF with every question, split, read and voice. When your session ends, Prometheus emails you the room's PDF read with a link back to the room.

FAQ Questions people ask

AI focus groups, in plain words.

What is an AI focus group?

A room of digital twins you can question like real people. In Crosswalk's Focus Group IQ, one seat stands for each archetype in the audience. Each seat answers in character from what its people did, and the count adds up the real people behind every answer.

Is a Crosswalk room better than a traditional focus group?

For knowing what an audience will do, yes. A traditional group hears a dozen recruited people, and each chair counts the same. A Crosswalk room seats every archetype in the audience and counts the real people behind each answer.

How is Focus Group IQ different from other AI focus group tools?

The seats come from 5+ years of real behavior on 10 million opted-in US people, not from personas you write. Answers are counted in real people and say how firm they are.

How many people are in an AI focus group?

One per archetype, and never fewer than 12. An audience with more archetypes gets more seats. You never pick the number.

Are the people in the room real?

Each seat is an archetype representative: a person who stands for one group inside the audience. The group is real people on Crosswalk's opted-in panel, and the seat answers from their 5+ years of behavior. No individual is identified.

How accurate is an AI focus group?

Every Crosswalk count carries a range and one of three words: firm (within about 5 points), leaning (about 12) or early (up to about 20). Calls are dated before the result and scored in public; the first six landed within 3.2% on average.

Do the answers change over time?

They can, because people change. The twin keeps learning from what its people do, so a question asked next month may get a different answer.

Can an AI focus group watch a video?

Yes. Pilot Testing seats the room in front of a pilot, an episode, an ad or a feature. Each seat turns a dial from 0 to 100 as it watches, reacts on the clock, and fills out a scorecard at the end.

How long does an AI focus group take?

Minutes. A question comes back as soon as the seats have answered, and the next question is one line away. A planned Predictive Analytics run of 5 to 8 questions finishes with a verdict and a PDF report.
