Glossary Digital Twins IQ

The words we use for digital twins, synthetic data and the panel.

Thirty short definitions, in plain words. A twin is an audience you can ask. An archetype is one group inside it. A seat is the person who speaks for that group in a room. The count adds up the real people behind each answer, and every count carries a confidence score. The rest is below, with a link to the page that explains each one.

Digital twinDigital twins
A live copy of a real audience that you can ask questions. Crosswalk builds one from the observed behavior of its people over 5+ years, not from surveys. Read what a digital twin audience is.
TwinDigital twins
Short for digital twin. An audience you can ask, built from 5+ years of what its people did. A twin can be a show's viewers, a service's subscribers, a star's followers, a brand's buyers, or a group you shape.
ArchetypeDigital twins
One group inside a twin, such as "older regulars on sport and crime series" or "parents who stopped after the season ended." A twin usually has 12 to 16 archetypes, found by sorting the audience by engagement stage and life stage. Together they add up to the whole audience.
Archetype representativeDigital twins
The person who stands for one archetype inside a twin, with a name, an age, a city and a bio. Each twin has 12 to 16. In a room, the archetype representative answers in character from what its people did.
SeatFocus groups
The landing spot for each archetype representative in a room. A seat has a bio and a memory. It remembers what it said earlier in the room and in past rooms you have used it in.
RoomFocus groups
A focus group with one seat for each archetype. Most rooms seat 12 to 16, never fewer than 12. You never pick the number; the audience decides it. Read how an AI focus group works.
Focus Group IQProducts
The part of Digital Twins IQ where you open a room on a twin and ask it anything. Each room comes back as a CSV and a PDF with every question, split, read and voice.
Predictive AnalyticsProducts
Bring one business question. Prometheus plans 5 to 8 questions from wide to narrow, runs the room for you, and hands back a verdict with a full report. Planning is free.
Pilot TestingProducts
The room watches your cut from start to finish: a pilot, an episode, an ad, a feature or a run of episodes. Seats turn a dial as they watch, react on the clock, answer questions at the turns of the story, and fill out a scorecard at the end.
The countFocus groups
The share, and the number of people, behind each answer. Each answer carries the people behind its seat, so a seat standing for 16.6 million people counts for more than one standing for 3.6 million. Shares add to 100% and people add to the room.
Confidence scoreFocus groups
Firm, leaning or early, with a range around the count. Firm is within about 5 points, leaning within about 12, early up to about 20. The room always makes the call; the word says how firm it is.
RangeFocus groups
How far the real number could sit from the count, shown as a low and a high. A count of 63.1% with a range of 51.2% to 75.0% is leaning.
VerdictFocus groups
The answer to a business question at the end of a Predictive Analytics run: yes, lean yes, lean no, no, split, or the winning option, with the numbers behind it, why, who drove it, what would change it, and what to ask next.
The dialPilot testing
The 0 to 100 line each seat turns while it watches a cut. It goes up when a scene lands and down when a scene drags, confuses or loses the seat. A reading every 2 seconds on a short cut and every 4 on a long one.
ReactionPilot testing
A moment marked on the clock by a seat: loved it, laughed, leaned in, lost, checked out or wow, each with its second and a line in the seat's own words.
ScorecardPilot testing
The card every seat fills out after a cut: overall rating, would recommend, would finish the episode, would watch a second episode, how soon, and against expectations. Each share counts the people behind the seats and carries a range and a confidence score.
Total positivePilot testing
Everyone who rates a cut excellent or very good. One of the four numbers at the top of a scorecard, next to excellent, definitely recommend and would finish.
The normPilot testing
What tests of the same kind usually score. You pick it, from all pilots to one genre such as procedurals. A genre gets its own norm once enough of its tests are on file.
Dead spotPilot testing
The second where the room's dial sank the most during a cut. The favorite moment is the strongest good reaction in the room.
Track recordAccuracy
The public record of every call the twins made before its outcome, dated and hashed, then scored when the result landed. Misses stay on the page. See the record.
CallAccuracy
A forecast or a room count with a real result to check against, put on the record before the result exists: the question, the audience, the figure and its range, the day the result is due and what will settle it. A call is never rewritten.
MissAccuracy
How far a call was off once the result landed. On a count, the share of the figure that happened. On a split, the points that would have to move for the two to match. On a winner, whether the side called was the side that won.
Behavioral panelThe panel
A group of people who agree to have what they do online recorded by software, so researchers can count real behavior instead of asking about it. Crosswalk's panel has 30 million opted-in people worldwide, 10 million in the US. Read what a behavioral panel is.
Single sourceThe panel
One person, one ID, every screen. The same person's phone, laptop and TV are counted together, so a search in the morning can be tied to a signup that night.
Census matchedThe panel
The US panel's mix of ages, genders, regions and the rest matches the country's, so every answer scales from the panel to 329.9 million Americans the way a census sample does.
Projected US peopleThe panel
A panel count scaled up to the US population. When a read says 6,184,372 US viewers, that is the panel's count projected to the country, each person counted once.
PenetrationThe panel
The share of an audience that did something: watched a service, bought a brand, searched a term. Always stated with its base.
IndexThe panel
How an audience compares to the US population, where 100 is the average American. An index of 683 means 6.8 times as common as among most people.
T+1The panel
Yesterday's behavior is on file today. The panel refreshes every morning.
Synthetic dataSynthetic data
Data that was produced rather than observed. At Crosswalk, the outcomes a twin produces when you run a plan it has never seen: new data, built on real behavior. Read synthetic data in market research.
Synthetic audienceSynthetic data
The twins a plan would reach, sized, described and projected to the US population. Made of the 12 to 16 archetypes that add up to the whole audience.
Synthetic respondentSynthetic data
A program that answers a survey as if it were a person. Most are built from past surveys or from a language model playing a type of person. Crosswalk's archetype representatives answer from behavior instead. Read the comparison.
PrometheusProducts
Crosswalk's research assistant. Ask it any audience question in plain English and it answers from what 10 million US people do. Ask it to build a twin, open a room, or explain a read.
Digital Twins IQProducts
Crosswalk's digital twin product, also called Twin IQ: the twins, Focus Group IQ, Predictive Analytics and Pilot Testing, with the public track record. See Digital Twins IQ.
CrosswalkCompany
Crosswalk Technologies, Inc., Malibu, California. An opted-in behavioral panel of 30 million people worldwide, 10 million in the US, read through Prometheus and the IQ reports. Clients receive group statistics; no individual is identified.
