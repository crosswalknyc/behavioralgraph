Guide The behavioral panel

What is a behavioral panel?

A behavioral panel is a group of people who agree to have what they do online recorded. Researchers can then count real behavior instead of asking about it. Crosswalk runs an opted-in behavioral panel of 30 million people worldwide, 10 million of them in the US. It sees each person's entire digital life across every screen, under one ID, day by day since January 2021. Clients receive group statistics projected to the US population. No individual is ever identified.

The short definition

A behavioral panel counts what people do. A survey panel asks what people think. Both are groups of people who agreed to take part. The difference is the instrument: software that records, or a questionnaire that asks.

On a behavioral panel, each person installs software and agrees to be counted. From then on, their online behavior is recorded as it happens. The sites, the apps, the searches, the streams, the purchases, each with the time of day. Nobody has to remember anything or type an answer. The record is what people did, not what they said.

What gets counted on the Crosswalk panel

An entire digital life, on every connected device. The same person on the phone, the laptop and the TV, under one ID, with URLs and timestamps.

  • Search. What people look up, and when.
  • Streaming video and music. What they watch and listen to, on which service, for how long.
  • Social. The platforms they open and the clips they watch.
  • Shopping. What they put in a cart and what they order.
  • Subscriptions. What they sign up for, what they pay, and what they cancel.
  • Apps, gaming, news, banking sessions, ticketing, and the rest of an ordinary day online.

Because the Crosswalk panel is single source, one question can follow one person from a search to a trailer to a signup to a purchase. That is why the same panel powers audience profiles, subscriber reads, journeys, attribution, and the digital twins.

How 10 million people stand for the country

The US panel is census matched. Every answer scales from the panel to the US population the way a census sample does, in percentages and in people.

A client never sees a panelist. They see group statistics. Penetration is the share of an audience that did something. An index compares the audience to the US population, where 100 is the average American. Projected US counts, 35 or more behavioral categories and ranked brands round it out. When Prometheus says 6,184,372 US viewers watched a Landman title in a window, that is the panel's count projected to 329.9 million Americans. Each person is counted once.

Freshness

Yesterday's behavior is on file today. The panel refreshes every morning.

The record runs continuously. A read on a premiere can be pulled the morning after it aired. A subscriber read can follow the same accounts week after week. And because the same people are on the panel year after year, their history stays intact: five years of it, since January 2021.

Consent, rights and privacy

Every panelist chose to be counted and can leave at any time. Identity is kept apart from behavior, and sensitive details are removed before anything is stored.

  • Opted in. Each person installed the Crosswalk software and agreed to it. The app explains what it collects before you install it.
  • Revocable. Uninstall the software and the collection stops. Ask, and the record is deleted.
  • De-identified for clients. Clients receive aggregated group statistics that cannot reasonably be linked back to a person. Read the de-identified data commitment.
  • Your choices. Your Privacy Choices, Opt-Out, tracking consent, and the Privacy Policy.

From the panel to a digital twin

A digital twin is an audience's 5+ years of behavior on the panel, turned into something you can ask.

Crosswalk builds a twin of any audience on the panel: a show's viewers, a brand's buyers, a star's followers, or a group you shape. The twin is made of 12 to 16 archetypes that add up to the whole audience. You seat it in a room, ask it anything, or play it a video. Read how Crosswalk builds a twin.

FAQ Questions people ask

The behavioral panel, in plain words.

What does behavioral panel mean?

A group of people who agree to have what they do online recorded by software. Researchers then count real behavior instead of asking about it. Crosswalk's panel has 30 million opted-in people worldwide and 10 million in the US.

How is a behavioral panel different from a survey panel?

A survey panel asks people what they think and what they remember doing. A behavioral panel records what they really did, as it happens, with the time of day. One asks; the other counts.

What does single source mean?

One person, one ID, every screen. The same person's phone, laptop and TV are counted together. A search in the morning can be tied to a signup that night without guessing.

How big is the Crosswalk panel?

30 million opted-in people worldwide, 10 million of them in the US. The US panel is census matched and projected to 329.9 million Americans.

When does the record start?

To January 2021. The same people are recorded day by day. An audience carries five years of history, and a twin rests on 5+ years of behavior.

Is the panel data current?

Yesterday's behavior is on file today. The panel refreshes every morning.

Can a client see an individual panelist?

No. Clients receive group statistics: penetration, an index against the US population, projected US counts and ranked brands. Identity is kept apart from behavior and no individual is identified.

How does someone join or leave the panel?

The panel app is free for Mac and Windows and explains what it collects before you install it. Uninstall it and the collection stops. Ask, and the record is deleted.
