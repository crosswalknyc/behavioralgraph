What is a digital twin audience?

A digital twin audience is a working copy of a real group of people that you can ask questions. It is built from what those people actually did, so it answers the way they would. Crosswalk builds a twin from 5+ years of observed behavior on an opted-in panel of 10 million US people. You ask the twin about a show, a price, a plan or a launch. It tells you what the audience will most likely do, with a range and a confidence score.

The short definition

A digital twin, in engineering, is a live digital copy of a machine. In audience research, a digital twin is a live digital copy of a group of people. You can test something on the twin before you spend money on the real audience.

The idea is simple. If you know what a group of people did for five years, you can ask what they would do next. A twin stores that history and answers from it. The answer is not a guess from a chatbot. It is a read of how people like that have behaved before.

A twin is not one average person

A real audience is made of different groups. A Crosswalk twin keeps those groups apart. Each group is an archetype, and each archetype has one person who speaks for it.

Take the people who subscribe to Amazon Prime Video in the US: 128,116,302 of them. Inside that audience are 16 archetypes. The biggest is older regulars who watch sport and crime series, about 1 in 8 of the audience. Another is under-35 fans of The Boys and Fallout, about 1 in 9. A smaller one is parents who stopped watching after the season ended, about 1 in 18. Each archetype gets a plain name and a person to stand for it, such as Devin, 65, from Tampa.

When you ask the twin a question, every archetype answers. Then the count adds up the real people behind each answer. An archetype of 16.6 million people counts for more than one of 3.6 million. That is why a twin can give you a percentage and a people count, not just a mood.

What a digital twin is built from

There are three ways a vendor can build a digital twin. The method decides what the twin can tell you.

Built fromWhat it is good atWhat it misses
Surveys and interviewsWhat people say they think and feelWhat people do when nobody is asking. People misremember and overstate.
A language model playing a type of personFast, cheap, endless questionsIt has never met the audience. It repeats the average of the internet.
Observed behavior over timeWhat people will do: watch, pay, cancel, return, buyPrivate feelings that never show up in behavior

Crosswalk twins are the third kind. Every twin is built from the record of what 10 million opted-in US people did across their entire digital life. That record holds what they watched and streamed, what they searched, what they bought, what they paid for and what they cancelled. It runs day by day since January 2021. Each person is one ID across their phone, laptop and TV.

What you can ask a digital twin

Anything you would ask the real audience, and some things you could not. You can also put a video in front of it and watch how it reacts.

  • A focus group question. "Which of these would you pay for?" The room answers, seat by seat, and the count adds up the people. See how an AI focus group works.
  • A business question. "If we raise the price $2, how many people leave?" Predictive Analytics plans 5 to 8 questions from wide to narrow. It runs the room and hands back a verdict with a report.
  • A cut of a show or an ad. Pilot Testing seats the room in front of your video. Each seat turns a dial from 0 to 100 as it watches, and fills out a scorecard at the end.

A real question on the Prime Video twin, November 18, 2026

"Prime Video can buy the rights to exactly one of these. Which one would get you to start paying, or keep paying?" The verdict: a new weeknight live sports package. 55.7% of the audience picked it, about 71 million people. That is about 4 times the 14.1% who said nothing on the list would make them pay.

How you know a digital twin is right

You check it against what really happened. Crosswalk dates every call before the result, scores it when the result lands, and keeps the misses on a public page.

Every count also carries a range and one of three words. Firm means the real number should sit within about 5 points. Leaning means within about 12. Early means a wide range, up to about 20 points, and the room still gives a verdict.

As of November 17, 2026, the public track record held six scored calls. All three calls on a winner were right. The counts landed within 3.2% of the result on average. The Texas Senate call, made four days out, was Talarico 54.6% to Paxton 43.3%. The certified result was 55.1% to 43.0%.

Digital twin vs persona vs synthetic respondent

These three words get mixed up. They are not the same thing.

  • A persona is a written sketch of a typical customer. It is a story, not data. It cannot answer a new question.
  • A synthetic respondent is a program that answers a survey as if it were a person. Most are built from surveys or from a language model. They tell you what people say.
  • A behavioral digital twin starts from the record of real actions. It tells you what people do, and it can be scored against real outcomes.

Who is in a digital twin

A Crosswalk twin stands in for people who chose to be counted. No individual is ever identified.

Every person in the panel installed the Crosswalk software and agreed to be counted. They can leave at any time. Identity is kept apart from behavior. FAQ Questions people ask

Plain answers on digital twin audiences.

What is a digital twin in market research?

A digital twin is a live copy of a real group of people that you can ask questions. In market research it stands in for a survey respondent or a focus group. Crosswalk builds one from 5+ years of observed behavior on an opted-in panel of 10 million US people.

How does a digital twin differ from a persona?

A persona is a written sketch of a typical customer. A digital twin is built from data on real people and can answer a new question. A Crosswalk twin also carries a range and a confidence score on every count.

Digital twin audience vs synthetic respondent

Most synthetic respondents are built from surveys or from a language model answering as a type of person. They report what people say. A behavioral digital twin rests on what people did, and it is scored against real outcomes.

How many archetypes are in a digital twin?

Usually 12 to 16, and never fewer than 12. The audience's own mix of engagement stage and life stage decides the number. An archetype smaller than 2.5% of the audience joins the one closest to it.

What can I test on a digital twin?

A show, a pilot, an ad, a trailer, a price, a promotion, a release week, a plan, or a bare idea. You can ask a question, run a planned set of questions, or play a video and watch the dial.

How accurate are digital twin audiences?

Crosswalk publishes every call before the result and scores it when the result lands. As of November 17, 2026, six public calls had been scored. All three winners were called right, and the counts landed within 3.2% of the result on average.

Does a digital twin use personal data?

Each twin stands in for people who chose to be counted. Identity is kept apart from behavior and no individual is identified. Clients receive group statistics projected to the US population.

How do I get a digital twin of my audience?

Ask Prometheus, Crosswalk's research assistant, to build one from a profile Crosswalk holds, from a group you shape, or from a list of links. A twin usually takes under an hour.
